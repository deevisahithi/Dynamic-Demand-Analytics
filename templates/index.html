01

Daily Demand Trend

This graph shows how total retail demand changes from day to day across the selected period. It helps identify increasing or decreasing demand trends, sudden demand increases, and unusual periods that may require closer inventory attention. The rolling average helps smooth daily fluctuations and makes the overall demand trend easier to understand.

02

Product Demand

This graph compares the total observed demand across different product families. It shows which product categories contribute the largest share of demand and which categories have relatively lower demand. This information can help retailers prioritize inventory planning for high-demand product categories.

03

Monthly Demand

This graph shows how total demand varies across different months. It helps identify recurring seasonal patterns and months with relatively high or low demand. These patterns can support advance inventory planning for periods where demand is expected to change significantly.

04

Yearly Demand

This graph compares total observed demand across different years in the historical dataset. It provides a long-term view of how retail demand has changed over time and helps identify broader increases, decreases, or relatively stable demand patterns.

05

Store Performance

This graph compares the total observed demand across stores. It identifies locations with higher and lower demand levels, showing that demand is not necessarily uniform across all stores. Store-level differences can help retailers plan inventory allocation according to local demand patterns.

06

Weekly Demand Pattern

This graph shows the average daily demand for each day of the week. It helps identify recurring weekly patterns, such as days with consistently higher or lower demand. Understanding these patterns can support short-term inventory and replenishment planning.

07

Promotion vs Demand

This graph compares the observed average demand for products associated with promotion activity and products without promotion activity. It helps identify whether demand levels differ between these groups in the historical data. The comparison shows an association in the data and should not be interpreted as proof that promotions directly caused the difference.

08

Holiday & Event Demand

This graph compares observed demand during holidays or special events with demand during regular days. It helps identify whether certain event periods are associated with noticeable changes in demand. These patterns can help retailers prepare inventory for periods with unusual demand behavior.

09

Demand Spikes

This graph highlights periods where observed demand is significantly higher than the normal demand level. These unusual increases can indicate periods that require closer inventory attention. The detected spikes are statistical demand signals and do not represent confirmed stockouts or inventory shortages.

Actual vs Predicted Demand

This graph compares the actual demand observed during the test period with the demand predicted by the Random Forest model. When the two lines follow similar patterns, the model is capturing the observed demand pattern more closely.

Feature Importance

This graph shows the relative importance of the input features used by the Random Forest model. Features with higher values contributed more to the model's predictions.

MODEL

Random Forest Regression

The model predicts numerical demand values rather than class labels. It combines multiple decision trees to capture relationships between demand and factors such as previous sales, time patterns, stores, product families and promotions.

TIME-AWARE EVALUATION

Historical Data → Recent Test Period

Earlier historical data is used for training and the most recent period is used for testing. This keeps the evaluation aligned with the real-world task of predicting future demand.
